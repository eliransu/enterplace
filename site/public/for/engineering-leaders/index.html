Use casesFor engineering leaders

"Can we roll out coding agents without losing track of them?"

You want every developer on a coding agent this quarter. Finance wants a number, security wants a record, and you do not want to pick one tool for everybody. Gantry sits under whichever tools your teams choose.

What changes for a developer

Close to nothing, which is the point.

Two settings, once
A base URL and a key, or one gantry login with the company account. The tool behaves as before.
Their own name on their work
Each session runs under the developer's key, inside the team's budget. Nobody shares a secret.
A refusal they can act on
When a budget or a rule stops a request, the reason shows up in the terminal they already use, with a decision ID.
The tool they chose
Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Copilot CLI, Gemini CLI and others go through the same gateway. Which tools were run against Gantry

What you can tell the others

Three conversations get shorter.

Who asksWhat they askWhat you show them
FinanceWhat will this cost?Spend per team and person, a monthly budget per team, and a forecast for each.
SecurityWhat are the agents sending out?A secrets guardrail that ran on trial first, and a record of every request with no prompt text in it.
Your own managersIs anyone using it?The coverage report: requests per person and tool, and who holds a key and has sent nothing.

Where it stops

  • Claude Code on Claude subscriptions goes through Gantry on its own login, and its limits are in tokens and requests, since Anthropic bills the seat. Dollar figures for those seats are estimates.
  • Windsurf, Amazon Q Developer and Kiro document no setting for a gateway of your own.
  • Gantry measures requests, tokens and cost. It does not measure whether the agents make your team faster.
