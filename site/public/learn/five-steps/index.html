LearnThe five steps

Five steps, one change at a time.

Each step is one change on top of the last, and each works without the next. Stay on a step for a day or a quarter. Nothing is blocked in the first two.

  1. See
  2. Know
  3. Limit
  4. Protect
  5. Prove
1

See

Which AI tools and models do we use, and what do they cost?

What you get

A list of the AI tools and models in use and what each one costs, from the first day. Nothing is blocked.

The one change

On the machine that will run Enterplace, run enterplace start. No provider key is needed: it writes a policy in observe mode with one rule that allows everyone, a free demo model and Claude subscription passthrough, and opens the dashboard. Its Start here checklist sends a first request and shows the settings for each coding tool. Then have the first people point their tool at the gateway.

Check it worked

enterplace start opens the console through a one-time sign-in link. The banner says Observe mode: nothing is blocked. The Analytics page ranks requests, tokens and cost by coding agent, model or key.

Who does it

A platform engineer. For everyone else it is one settings change in each tool.

Step 1 in the docs
2

Know

Who uses what, by person and team?

What you get

Every request has a name and a team on it. Usage and cost show up per person and per team.

The one change

Give each person a key of their own, in place of the shared one from step 1. The command prints the key once, with the lines their tool reads. With more than a handful of people, let them sign in with the company account and issue their own.

Check it worked

Analytics ranks by person or team. The People and workloads page lists every key by name and team, with what it spent this month and today. enterplace audit coverage names the people who hold a key and have sent nothing.

Who does it

The same engineer, or your identity team. People swap a shared key for their own. Nothing is blocked in this step either.

Step 2 in the docs
3

Limit

What is the most we can spend this month, and can we stop it today?

What you get

A cap on what each team spends in a month, and a stop button for a person, a team, a key or a model.

The one change

Add a budget for every team. Pick the amount from what step 2 showed you. Observe mode is still on, so a request over the budget is forwarded and recorded as would_refuse. Its cost still counts, so the numbers are real on the day you enforce.

Enforce it

When the amounts look right, delete the mode: observe line. From the next request, a team that reaches its budget is refused with budget_exceeded, and the message says when the budget starts again.

Who does it

You pick the amount. The engineer adds one line. For people, nothing changes until a team reaches its budget.

Step 3 in the docs
4

Protect

What stops a password from being sent to a model?

What you get

A prompt that holds an API key, a private key or a database password is stopped before it leaves. You see what would be stopped before anything is.

The one change

Add a guardrail and name it in your rule. Until the date, it records what it finds and blocks nothing. From that date it refuses a request that holds a secret with guardrail_blocked.

Check it worked

In the request log, filter by guardrail finding, such as aws_access_key or database_url. During the trial each finding says shadow. If the findings are real secrets, let the date arrive. If it catches text it should leave alone, move the date.

Who does it

Security picks the date. The engineer adds the lines. The detector runs inside Enterplace, and the text it matched is never written to a record.

Step 4 in the docs
5

Prove

Can we show an auditor every request, and that the record is complete?

What you get

One record for every request, allowed or refused, kept where a restart cannot lose it, with a check that shows no record was changed.

The one change

Enterplace has written an audit record for every request since step 1. Put the file on storage that outlives the container: mount a volume, move the file onto it, add one line to the policy, and restart once.

Check it worked

Run the verify command, or press Verify on the console's Audit log page. Copy the last hash to a place the Enterplace host cannot write, such as a ticket. Later, --expect-last shows that nothing up to that record was changed or removed.

With Enterplace Enterprise Enterprise

Export of every record to a SIEM, retention that keeps the chain verifiable, and a legal hold.

Step 5 in the docs

After step five, you can answer all five.

The questions from the start, and where each answer now comes from.

  • Which tools and models do we use, and what do they cost? The Analytics page, by tool, model and key.
  • Who uses what? The same page by person and team, and the coverage report.
  • What is the most we can spend, and can we stop it? The budgets in the policy, and the kill switch.
  • What stops a password from being sent? The secrets guardrail, with its findings in the request log.
  • Can we show every request, and that the record is complete? The audit log and the verify command.
