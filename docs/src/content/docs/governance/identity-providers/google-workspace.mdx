Google Workspace can sign people in to the admin console with OpenID Connect (`console.sso`) or SAML 2.0 (`console.saml`). Google's ID token carries no groups, so with OpenID Connect you give roles by email address. SAML can send Google group membership, so use SAML when roles should follow groups.

The examples use `https://gantry.example.com` for your Gantry address and `example.com` for your Workspace domain.

## OpenID Connect

<Steps>

1. In the Google Cloud console, select or create a project for Gantry. Fill in the **Branding** page. On the **Audience** page, choose **Internal**, so only accounts in your organization can use the client (Google offers it to Workspace organizations only).

2. On the **Clients** page, click **Create client** and choose a web application.

3. Under authorized redirect URIs, add `https://gantry.example.com/console/sso/callback`. The value must match exactly. `gantry login` comes back on the same URI.

4. Copy the client ID, and copy or download the client secret now: Google shows the full secret only once, when it is created.

</Steps>

Google's ID token has `email`, `email_verified` and `hd` (the Workspace domain) but no groups claim. Google documents its `iss` as either `https://accounts.google.com` or `accounts.google.com`; with `issuer: https://accounts.google.com`, Gantry takes both. Use `email` as the role claim and list people by address:

```yaml title="gantry.policy.yaml"
console:
  sso:
    issuer: https://accounts.google.com
    client_id: 123456789012-abc123def456ghi789jkl012mno345pq.apps.googleusercontent.com
    client_secret_env: GANTRY_SSO_SECRET
    redirect_url: https://gantry.example.com/console/sso/callback
    allowed_domains: [example.com]
    role_claim: email
    admin_values: [ana@example.com, ben@example.com]
    read_only_values: [chris@example.com, dana@example.com]
```

`allowed_domains` keeps out Google accounts from other domains. To give everyone in the domain read-only access instead, set `role_claim: hd` and `read_only_values: [example.com]`; admins then sign in with an admin Gantry key. Set the secret where Gantry runs: `GANTRY_SSO_SECRET=...`.

<Aside type="note">
With `role_claim: email`, a team's `sso_values` and a role's `sso_values` are email addresses too. For `gantry login`, list developers in a team's `members` or set `self_issue.team`. When group membership should drive roles and teams, use SAML 2.0 below.
</Aside>

## SAML 2.0

<Steps>

1. In the Google Admin console, as a super administrator, go to **Menu** > **Apps** > **Web and mobile apps** and click **Add App** > **Add custom SAML app**. Name it, then **Continue**.

2. On **Google Identity Provider details**, copy the **SSO URL** and **Entity ID**, and download the **Certificate**. Then **Continue**.

3. In **Service Provider Details**:
   - **ACS URL**: `https://gantry.example.com/console/saml/acs`
   - **Entity ID**: `https://gantry.example.com/console/saml/metadata`
   - **Start URL**: leave empty
   - **Signed response**: leave unchecked (Google then signs the assertion) or check it to sign the whole response as well
   - **Name ID format**: EMAIL, with **Name ID** the primary email

4. In **Attribute mapping**, click **Add mapping**, choose **Primary email** and name the app attribute `email`.

5. Under **Group membership**, add the Google groups that give console access (for example `gantry-admins` and `gantry-viewers`) and enter `groups` as the **App attribute**. Google sends only the listed groups the person belongs to, directly or through another group. You can list up to 75 groups.

6. Click **Finish**. Then open the app, click **User access**, and turn it **On** for the organizational units or access group that need the console. The change can take up to 24 hours.

</Steps>

Google's documentation does not say whether a group is sent by its name or its email address. If a member of a listed group is refused with `sso_role` while the name is right, put the group's email address in the policy instead. After renaming a group, add it again under **Group membership** so the new name is sent, and update the policy.


{/* not checked: the certificate is a placeholder */}
```yaml title="gantry.policy.yaml"
console:
  saml:
    idp_entity_id: https://accounts.google.com/o/saml2?idpid=C01abcd2e
    idp_sso_url: https://accounts.google.com/o/saml2/idp?idpid=C01abcd2e
    idp_certificate: |
      -----BEGIN CERTIFICATE-----
      MIIDdDCCAlygAwIBAgIGAY...
      -----END CERTIFICATE-----
    sp_entity_id: https://gantry.example.com/console/saml/metadata
    acs_url: https://gantry.example.com/console/saml/acs
    email_attribute: email
    role_attribute: groups
    admin_values: [gantry-admins]
    read_only_values: [gantry-viewers]
    allowed_domains: [example.com]
```

Copy the **Entity ID** and **SSO URL** exactly as the Admin console shows them. Do not test with **Test SAML login** in the Admin console: it starts an IdP-initiated sign-in, which Gantry refuses. Test from `/console`.

## Teams and custom roles

The group names feed a custom role's `sso_values` under `roles:` and a team's `sso_values` under `teams:`. At `gantry login`, a person listed in a team's `members` gets that team, otherwise the first team whose `sso_values` match one of their groups, otherwise `self_issue.team`.

## Check it

<Steps>

1. Open `https://gantry.example.com/console` in a private window and click the sign-in button.

2. Sign in with an account listed in `admin_values` (OpenID Connect) or in the admin group (SAML). The console opens with the admin role.

3. Sign in with a read-only account. The console opens read only.

</Steps>

A refusal with `sso_role` means the email or group values did not match the policy. With SAML, check that the group is listed under **Group membership** in the app and that its name in the policy is exact.
