## Five steps

Your people already use AI tools. Enterplace sits between those tools and the model providers, and takes you from seeing what is in use to governing it, one change at a time. Step 1 blocks nothing.

| Step | What you do | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| [1. See](/start/step-1-see/) | Run `enterplace start` and point your tools at it | Which tools and models are in use and what they cost |
| [2. Know](/start/step-2-know/) | Give each person their own key | A name and a team on every request |
| [3. Limit](/start/step-3-limit/) | Set a budget per team | A cap on spend, and a stop button |
| [4. Protect](/start/step-4-protect/) | Add a check for secrets, on trial first | Keys and passwords stay out of prompts |
| [5. Prove](/start/step-5-prove/) | Keep and verify the audit log | Evidence of every request |

## How it works

Coding agents (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Copilot), your own apps and agents, MCP clients, and any OpenAI, Anthropic or Gemini SDK point at Enterplace instead of the provider. Behind it are OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Vertex AI, Bedrock, Azure OpenAI, Mistral and local models on Ollama, vLLM or LM Studio. Enterplace identifies the caller, decides by policy, writes an audit record chained to the one before it (so a removed record shows), and only then forwards the request with the provider key in place of the caller's.

It ships as one binary, or one container image. Policy, keys and the audit log are plain files you can review, version and verify; Postgres is optional, for teams that share keys and budgets across gateways.

## Governance

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Identity on every request" icon="seti:lock">
		Each person and workload gets an Enterplace key tied to a name and a team. Only its SHA-256 is stored. Developers can issue their own expiring key through SSO with `enterplace login`.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Policy as code" icon="document">
		First-match rules by team, person or anyone decide which models a caller may use. `enterplace check` rejects unknown fields and names the file, line and fix.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Tamper-evident audit log" icon="list-format">
		One hash-chained record per request, written before forwarding. Refusals are recorded too. `enterplace audit verify` proves nothing was changed or reordered.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Budgets and rate limits" icon="seti:pipeline">
		Dollar budgets per organization, team, person, key, end customer, tag or model, reserved at admission so they cannot be overrun. Requests and tokens per minute, hour and day, with `Retry-After`.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Guardrails" icon="warning">
		Built-in secret and personal data detectors, thirteen guardrail vendors, and your own services by webhook. Block, mask or record, on requests and answers.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Kill switches" icon="error">
		Stop a person, a team, a key or a model at once with `enterplace kill`. The person stopped sees who set it and why.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## Routing

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Every major provider" icon="puzzle">
		Over 120 provider presets: OpenAI, Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, Amazon Bedrock (every model family), Vertex AI, Gemini, Cohere, IBM watsonx.ai, SageMaker, Replicate, and any OpenAI-compatible server: Groq, Mistral, Together, Fireworks, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Retries, fallbacks and aliases" icon="random">
		Retries and a fallback per provider. Model aliases spread one name over several deployments by round robin, least busy, lowest latency, lowest cost or adaptive, with cooldown and named fallbacks.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Cross-API translation" icon="translate">
		Codex's Responses requests reach any provider. Opt in to send chat completions to Claude, or Claude Code's messages requests to GPT, Gemini or a local model.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Cost on every answer" icon="seti:db">
		Tokens and cost recorded for each answer, with prices built in for most models. `enterplace prices <model>` shows the price and its source.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>
